Copyright & DMCA Policy

Last updated: August 3, 2026
Draft pending legal review. This page sets out our intended notice-and-takedown procedure and is written to follow the requirements of the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act. It has not yet been reviewed by an attorney. If you need to send a notice today, use the contact details below and we will act on it.

1. Our position

The Peripheral Initiative exists to help independent artists and labels get heard. Taking other people's work without permission is the opposite of that, and we take it seriously.

We host material submitted by our users — forum posts, discussion messages, photographs of record shops, and text describing music. We do not host recorded music. If that ever changes, this page will be updated before it does.

We respond to valid notices of claimed copyright infringement, we remove or disable access to material where appropriate, and we terminate the accounts of repeat infringers.

2. Reporting claimed infringement

If you own a copyright, or are authorised to act for the owner, and you believe material on our services infringes it, send a written notice to our designated agent. To be effective under the DMCA, your notice must include all of the following:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work you say has been infringed. If you are covering multiple works in a single notice, a representative list is acceptable.
  3. Identification of the material you say is infringing, with enough detail for us to find it — a direct URL is best, plus a username, thread title, or timestamp where relevant.
  4. Your contact details: name, mailing address, telephone number, and email address.
  5. A statement that you have a good faith belief the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in your notice is accurate, and — under penalty of perjury — that you are the copyright owner or authorised to act on the owner's behalf.
A notice missing any of these six elements may not be effective, and we may not be able to act on it. If yours is incomplete we will usually tell you what is missing rather than simply ignoring it.

4. What happens after we receive a notice

  • We review the notice for the six elements above.
  • If it is effective, we remove or disable access to the material identified, generally without delay.
  • We notify the user who posted it, pass on a copy of the notice, and explain how to file a counter-notification.
  • We record the notice against that account for the purposes of our repeat infringer policy.

5. Counter-notification

If your material was removed and you believe that was a mistake, or that the material was misidentified, you may send a counter-notification. It must include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed and the location where it appeared before removal.
  3. A statement, under penalty of perjury, that you have a good faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, mailing address and telephone number, together with a statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the federal district court for the district in which you live — or, if you live outside the United States, any judicial district in which we may be found — and that you will accept service of process from the person who sent the original notice.

If we receive an effective counter-notification, we will forward it to the person who sent the original notice and let them know we may restore the material in 10 to 14 business days, unless they tell us they have filed a court action seeking to restrain the activity. We will then restore the material unless we hear that such an action has been filed.

6. Repeat infringers

We terminate accounts that are the subject of repeated valid notices. Because our services include a username permanently tied to a single verified contact method, a terminated account cannot simply be re-registered under the same name.

Open decision for review: the specific threshold and lookback window for “repeat” are not yet fixed, and how they are defined affects our safe-harbour position. This is one of the points we are seeking advice on.

7. Misrepresentation

Under the DMCA, a person who knowingly materially misrepresents that material is infringing — or that it was removed by mistake — may be liable for damages, including costs and legal fees. Please do not use this process to remove material you simply dislike, or to interfere with a competitor.

8. Trade marks and other rights

This page covers copyright. For trade mark concerns, or any other intellectual property matter, write to lachlan@theperipheralinitiative.com and describe the right you hold and what you would like us to do.

9. Changes to this policy

We will update this page when our services change, and always before we begin hosting any new category of material. The date at the top reflects the most recent revision.
