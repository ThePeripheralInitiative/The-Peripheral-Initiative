Policy

Community Guidelines

Last updated: August 3, 2026 · Applies to the Peripheral forum and to Dis-Go
Draft pending legal review. These are the rules we intend to enforce and they reflect how the products are actually built. The wording has not yet been reviewed by an attorney.

The short version. This is a place for people who care about music. Talk to each other the way you would in a good record shop — like the other person is a real person who was there first.

Be honest about what you have found and where. Do not use other people as material. If something is wrong, report it; if you were wrong, say so.

Who can be here

You must be 18 or older to hold an account. This is not negotiable and it applies to both the forum and Dis-Go.

One person, one account. Your username is permanently tied to one verified email address or phone number. Creating additional accounts to get around a restriction, inflate a leaderboard, or vote on your own submissions is not allowed.

Your username

Your username is the first thing anyone sees, and for most people it is the only thing they will ever know about you. It cannot be hateful, harassing, sexually explicit, or chosen to impersonate another person, a real business, or our staff.

Names are checked automatically at signup and can be reported afterwards. If a name is found to break these rules you will be asked to change it before you can keep using your account, and you can appeal that decision.

Store discussions

Every discussion thread in Dis-Go belongs to a specific shop, not to whoever happens to be standing nearby. That is a deliberate design decision: we do not want a feature that lets someone work out where another person physically is.

There are no private messages in Dis-Go, and we have no plans to add them. If we ever reconsider, it will not happen quietly.

In a store thread: talk about the shop and the music. Answer people's questions about stock if you know the answer. Do not use a shop's thread to attack the shop, its staff, or another user.

Photographs you submit

Dis-Go asks for live photographs in a few places — adding a shop, reporting one as closed, or verifying a check-in when you have no signal. Every one of those photographs is reviewed before anyone other than you can see it.

  • Photograph the storefront, not the people outside it.
  • Avoid capturing faces, vehicle plates, or anything through a window that someone would not expect to be published.
  • Never photograph the inside of a shop without asking. It is their space.
  • Do not submit a photograph you did not take.

Being accurate about shops

Real businesses depend on what this app says about them. Getting it wrong costs them customers.

  • Only add a shop that exists, and only from outside it, in a public and well-lit place. If somewhere feels unsafe, do not go in and do not add it — trust that instinct.
  • Only report a shop as closed if you saw that it was. Closure reports need confirmation from several separate people and a moderator's review before anything changes, but the process starts with you telling the truth.
  • Do not mark a competitor's shop as closed. If you have a commercial interest in a listing, say so rather than acting on it.
  • If you flag something as unsafe, mean it. That flag pauses check-ins at a real business immediately.

Playing fair

The point of Dis-Go is that you actually went somewhere. Everything in the scoring assumes that.

  • No faked location. Spoofing GPS is detected, earns nothing, and is flagged.
  • No claiming purchases you did not make. Store rewards come out of a shop's own margin. Submitting a purchase you cannot back up may cost you your standing at that shop, and doing it deliberately or repeatedly may cost you your account.
  • Browsing without buying is completely fine. That is what record shops are for. It earns you points like anything else. Only the shop's own rewards require a real purchase.
  • Do not automate anything. Scripts, emulators, and modified clients are not welcome.

Reporting, and reporting honestly

You can report a username, a message, or a shop listing. Reports go to a moderator, not into a void, and you will not be told who reported what about whom.

Getting it wrong in good faith costs you nothing. If you report something that turns out to be fine, and it was a reasonable mistake, nothing happens to you. Reporting real problems should never feel risky. What does count against you is reporting things you know are fine — that carries warnings, and repeated abuse of the report system can end an account.

Never allowed

No warnings, no second chances
  • Harassment, threats, or targeting a person
  • Hate speech or slurs of any kind
  • Stalking, or trying to work out where someone physically is
  • Sexual content, and anything at all involving minors
  • Doxxing or publishing anyone's private information
  • Encouraging self-harm
  • Coordinated attacks on a person, a shop, or a listing
  • Selling accounts, standing, or rewards

What happens when a rule is broken

Step 01
A moderator reviews it. Not an automated ban. A person looks at the report and the context.
Step 02
The response fits the problem. That might mean removing a message, requiring a username change, pausing a feature, or removing an account. Items in the list above skip straight to the end.
Step 03
You are told what happened and why. A suspended account can still sign in far enough to read the decision and appeal it.
Step 04
You can appeal, and a different moderator reviews it. Never the one who made the original call. If the appeal succeeds, the action is reversed.
Step 05
We watch the moderators too. How often a moderator's decisions get overturned on appeal is tracked, and a pattern of overturns is escalated for review.

Shops and their own threads

A verified shop can claim its listing, correct its details, and speak in its own thread with a badge showing it is really them.

Shops cannot moderate their own thread. The reason people trust a store thread is that it tells them what is genuinely on the shelf, including when the website said otherwise. An owner who could delete that would remove the only thing making it useful. Shops can report content like anyone else.

Copyright

Do not post work that is not yours to post. Our full notice-and-takedown procedure is on the Copyright & DMCA page.

Changes

These guidelines will change as the products do. Substantive changes will be announced rather than slipped in, and the date at the top always reflects the current version.
